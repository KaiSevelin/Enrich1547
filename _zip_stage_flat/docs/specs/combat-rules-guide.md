# Combat Rules Guide

## Purpose

This document explains the current `1547Core` combat rules in plain language.

It is intended to be readable by designers, testers, and players who need to
understand how combat works without reading the state-machine and automation
specifications.

## Combat Overview

Combat is side-based.

At the start of combat, initiative is rolled once for each side. The side with
the better result acts first. Initiative is not rerolled every round.

After that:

- one side becomes active
- actors on that side may act in flexible order
- when a player on that side chooses `End Group Turn`, the other side acts
- when both sides have completed activation, a new round begins

The same initiative order is used again on the new round.

## What A Side Activation Means

When a side is active, its actors are not locked into a strict one-after-another
turn sequence.

Instead:

- any actor on the active side may act
- movement can be split across multiple move actions
- an actor can attack, move, and attack again if they still have attacks
- the side remains active until the group chooses `End Group Turn`

This means the active side can coordinate actions freely within its activation
window.

## Actor Action Economy

Each actor tracks what they still have available during the round.

The important parts are:

- movement budget
- remaining attacks
- whether a full-turn maneuver is still available
- which maneuvers have already been used this turn
- current `RiskPoints`

Actors may also be marked `done` as a coordination aid, but this does not
formally end the side's activation.

Action economy is consumed when an action commits, not when it is merely
declared.

That means:

- declaring an action does not spend it yet
- if the action fails before commit, the action economy is still available

## Action Types

There are 3 main action types:

- move
- attack
- full-turn maneuver

Pre-maneuvers are not separate actions. They are attached to an attack when that
attack is declared.

Post-maneuvers happen after an attack has resolved and use `CriticalPoints`
generated by the roll.

Reactions are special maneuvers that trigger when specific conditions are met.

## Move Actions

Movement is tracked in squares.

An actor may spend movement in multiple separate move actions while their side is
active.

Combat range on the square grid uses Chebyshev distance by default.

That means:

- distance between two squares is `max(abs(dx), abs(dy))`
- orthogonal and diagonal adjacency both count as distance `1`
- from `(x, y)`, the squares `(x+1, y)`, `(x, y+1)`, and `(x+1, y+1)` are all
  distance `1`
- from `(x, y)`, the squares `(x+2, y+1)` and `(x+2, y+2)` are both distance
  `2`

All combat reach and range checks should use this metric unless a later rule
explicitly overrides it.

Threat areas depend on the actor's current facing and the reach of the equipped
weapon.

Example:

- an actor facing `N` with a Reach `1` weapon threatens `NW`, `N`, and `NE`
- an actor facing `N` with a Reach `2` weapon threatens 2 squares out on `NW`,
  `N`, and `NE`
- an actor facing `N` with `minReach: 2`, `maxReach: 3` threatens 3 squares out
  on `NW`, `N`, and `NE`, but does not threaten the squares immediately
  adjacent to the actor
- that same actor is at a defense disadvantage against attacks from `SW`, `S`,
  and `SE`

Important rules:

- movement is resolved square by square
- an actor may rotate freely during its side activation without spending
  movement unless a later rule says otherwise
- standing up from `prone` costs `2` squares of movement
- entering a threatened square commits movement into that square
- if a reaction triggers there, it resolves from that position
- if movement is interrupted, only the distance already moved is committed

This lets movement interact naturally with threat zones and reaction windows.

## Attack Actions

An attack must have at least:

- attack dice
- an attack type
- a target or an array of targets

Most attacks will have one target, but group attacks are allowed.

An attack goes through this general flow:

1. the attack is declared
2. any pre-maneuvers are attached
3. pre-maneuver costs are reserved
4. reactions may modify the attack
5. attacker and defender roll simultaneously
6. damage is applied
7. damage-taken reactions may resolve
8. post-maneuvers may be used
9. reserved points are either spent or released
10. the action commits

## Pre-Maneuvers

Pre-maneuvers are declared as part of an attack before the roll happens.

They usually do things like:

- add dice
- force a safe attack
- ignore certain die types
- ignore certain rolled results

Their costs are reserved first. They are not spent immediately.

If the attack commits and the pre-maneuver was actually used:

- the reservation becomes spent

If the attack does not commit or the pre-maneuver effect is not used:

- the reservation is released

## Safe Attacks

A safe attack is still a normal attack roll, but with fewer side effects.

Safe attacks:

- do not generate crits
- do not generate fumbles
- do not generate reactions from that attack

A safe attack does not have an inherent bonus or penalty by itself. If the
maneuver that created the safe attack also changes dice or modifiers, those
changes still apply.

For group attacks, all targets inherit the same safe-attack rules.

## Reactions

Reactions are maneuvers that trigger when something specific happens.

Examples of triggers include:

- an actor entering a threat zone
- an attack being declared
- damage being taken

Important reaction rules:

- reactions do not trigger further reactions
- a reaction maneuver can only be used once per turn per maneuver type
- reactions usually change an action rather than cancel it outright

Movement-triggered reactions are one unified reaction family.

Within that family:

- some maneuvers trigger because movement crossed a legal reaction point
- some maneuvers suppress movement-triggered reactions for an attached movement
- some maneuvers alter the outcome of the triggered movement reaction

Typical reaction effects include:

- forcing a safe attack
- improving defense
- applying a stunt such as disarm
- stopping or altering movement

Examples:

- `Opportunity Strike` triggers from movement
- `Hold At Bay` stops movement when its reaction resolves
- `Disengage` suppresses movement-triggered reactions for the attached movement
- `Advance Under Guard` suppresses `Opportunity Strike` for the attached
  movement

If more than one reaction is legal in the same window:

1. the reacting side chooses which legal reaction to resolve
2. that chosen reaction resolves fully
3. no further reactions are generated by that reaction

If multiple actors on the same side could react, that side chooses their order.

## Stunts

Stunts are only automated if a specific stunt handler exists.

If no handler exists:

- the stunt can still exist as valid content
- it remains a narrative or GM-adjudicated effect instead of a fully automated
  one

At the moment, `disarm` is the main explicit stunt example in the specs.

## Dice And Roll Results

Combat dice produce totals in these categories:

- `damage`
- `protection`
- `crit`
- `fumble`
- `multiplier`

Attack and defense are rolled simultaneously.

After rolls are made:

- result filters may ignore some dice or rolled results
- multiplier is applied to `damage`, `protection`, and `crit`
- multiplier does not apply to `fumble`

Damage is then calculated as:

```js
damageApplied = Math.max(0, attackerDamage - defenderProtection)
```

## Group Attacks

For a group attack:

- the attacker rolls once
- every target faces that same attack roll
- each target rolls defense separately
- each target resolves its own damage and defender-side crit/fumble generation

This keeps group attacks consistent while still allowing each target to defend
individually.

## Criticals And Post-Maneuvers

Both attacker and defender can generate crits.

Crits become `CriticalPoints` for that attack resolution.

After damage and any damage-taken reactions:

- the defender spends post-maneuvers first
- the attacker spends post-maneuvers second

When the post-maneuver window closes:

- any remaining `CriticalPoints` from that attack are set to `0`

## Fumbles, RiskPoints, And Risk Dice

Fumbles on a roll generate `RiskPoints`.

`RiskPoints` are stored on the actor.

On that actor's next attack or defense roll in the same combat:

- each `RiskPoint` becomes one `risk` die
- all stored `RiskPoints` are consumed on that one roll

After that roll:

- `RiskPoints` are set to `0`

When combat ends:

- any remaining `RiskPoints` are set to `0`

## Full-Turn Maneuvers

A full-turn maneuver uses up the actor's normal move and attack availability when
it commits.

These maneuvers:

- do not trigger normal movement or attack reactions by themselves
- may create persistent effects

An example is Overwatch.

Full-turn effects last until that same side becomes active again.

## Prepared Effects

Prepared effects are setup states that grant a later tactical benefit.

The current prepared effects are:

- `aimed`
- `braced`
- `overwatch`

Shared prepared-effect rules:

- they are gained by a setup maneuver rather than by an ordinary attack roll
- they provide a later benefit while they remain active
- they are spoiled if the owner becomes the target of an attack unless a later
  rule explicitly says otherwise

## Escape And Break Free

Some control effects require a later escape attempt rather than ending
automatically.

The current escape procedure is:

1. the controlled actor spends a later action to attempt escape
2. the actor and the controller make an opposed roll
3. each side may use `Strength` or `Dexterity` if the effect allows that choice
4. if the controlled actor succeeds, the control effect ends
5. if the controlled actor fails, the control effect remains active

`Lock` and `Choke` currently use this procedure unless a later rule explicitly
overrides it.

## Maneuver Costs

For `pre`, `reaction`, and `full-turn` maneuvers, allowed cost types are:

- `StrengthPoints`
- `StaminaPoints`
- `DexterityPoints`
- `CharismaPoints`
- `IntelligencePoints`
- `FaithPoints`
- `PowerPoints`
- `null`

For `post` maneuvers:

- `CostType` must be `CriticalPoints`

If a maneuver has no cost:

- `CostType` should be `null`
- `CostAmount` should be `0`

## Persistent Effects

Some maneuvers create effects that last beyond a single action.

These are persistent effects.

An example is Overwatch, which creates a reaction-ready state that remains active
until it expires.

Persistent effects are tracked separately from a one-off roll or one-off action.

## HUD And Play Flow

The combat HUD should support the rules without replacing them.

The intended HUD structure is:

- `Attack`
- `Defense`
- `Movement`
- `Control`
- `Support`
- `Special`

These are player-facing presentation groups.

They do not replace the more detailed internal categories used by content or
implementation.

Important HUD rules:

- `Attack` centers on equipped weapons and attack-building actions
- `Defense` centers on reactions, guard actions, and defensive status awareness
- `Movement` centers on movement budget, facing, and movement-safe maneuvers
- `Control` centers on locks, pushes, turns, and battlefield-position effects
- `Support` centers on ally-helping maneuvers and command-style actions
- `Special` covers powers, unusual item use, and non-core combat actions

The HUD should also include:

- a combat header showing round and active side
- a clear `End Group Turn` control
- token-facing visualization so players can read front and rear positioning
  clearly
- a temporary threat-zone overlay for the selected or active actor
- a distinct backstab or rear-disadvantage overlay for the actor's rear arc
- movement-path warnings when a path enters an enemy threat zone
- reaction prompts that interrupt clearly when needed
- post-maneuver prompts that resolve defender first, then attacker

The HUD is a play surface and input layer. It is not the rules engine.

In Foundry, these zones should normally be shown as temporary board overlays
instead of permanent map art.

Recommended presentation:

- show threat and rear-arc overlays when a token is selected, hovered, moving,
  or targeting an attack
- rotate those overlays with the token's facing
- use one subtle color family for threat and another for rear disadvantage or
  backstab
- all combat overlays should occupy complete tiles only
- avoid wedges, cones, diagonal slices, or partial-tile indicators for these
  overlays
- keep tile fills low-opacity so the board remains readable underneath
- when a tile is the exact tile that triggers a reaction window or a legal
  backstab, strengthen that tile slightly instead of switching to a louder
  effect style
- show dead-zone gaps clearly for weapons such as `minReach: 2`, `maxReach: 3`

### Threat And Rear Overlay Style

Threat and rear feedback should be subtle and tile-based.

Recommended style:

- threat zone feedback uses full-tile fills only
- rear or backstab feedback uses full-tile fills only
- threat and rear tiles should remain visually distinct, but both should be
  understated
- if a tile is both a legal attack tile and a legal rear-attack tile, intensify
  that tile slightly rather than adding a new overlay shape

This keeps the board readable and ensures players can parse the information at
a glance without the screen filling up with strong combat graphics.

## Automation Philosophy

The automation model is designed to stay controlled and predictable.

The important principles are:

- content data declares intent
- handlers return structured outcomes
- core resolvers apply mutations and commit state
- handlers should not directly mutate combat documents arbitrarily

This keeps combat rules easier to validate, test, and debug.

## What Ends A Side Activation

A side activation ends when a player on the active side uses the `End Group Turn`
control in the HUD.

This is important:

- the side does not end just because one actor has finished
- actor `done` markers are only coordination tools unless later rules say
  otherwise
- the active side remains active until the group chooses to end it

## What Starts A New Round

A new round starts after every side in the current round has completed activation.

When a new round starts:

- round number increases
- actor round state resets
- the first side in the stored initiative order activates again

Initiative itself does not reroll.

## Summary

The combat model is built around:

- one initiative roll per side at combat start
- side-based activation instead of strict actor turns
- flexible action order within the active side
- reactions that interrupt or modify actions
- simultaneous attack and defense rolls
- `CriticalPoints` for post-maneuvers
- `RiskPoints` that carry fumbles forward into the next roll

This gives the game a coordinated, tactical feel while still keeping the rules
structured enough for automation.
